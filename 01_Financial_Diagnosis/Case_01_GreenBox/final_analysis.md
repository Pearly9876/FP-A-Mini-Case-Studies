# Case 01 — Final Analysis

## Key Observation

GreenBox delivered strong revenue growth of approximately 26.9%, supported by growth in active customers, order frequency, and average revenue per order.

However, operating profit declined from $2.0m to $0.9m, while operating margin fell from 12.5% to approximately 4.4%. This indicates that revenue growth has not translated into stronger profitability.

## Revenue Drivers

Revenue growth was supported by three main drivers:

- Active customers increased by 15%
- Average orders per customer increased by 6.3%
- Average revenue per order increased by 4%

Customer growth was the largest individual driver, supported by higher purchasing frequency and revenue per order.

## Cost Diagnosis

Food and packaging, delivery, and marketing costs all increased faster than revenue.

Food and packaging cost per order increased from approximately $25.00 to $28.64, suggesting that the increase cannot be explained by higher order volume alone.

This may indicate higher input prices, product mix changes, packaging inflation, or operational inefficiency.

## Areas to Investigate

Management should investigate:

1. Food and packaging cost per order and its underlying drivers.
2. Customer acquisition cost and customer quality by marketing channel.
3. Delivery cost per order and operational measures such as orders per route or distance per delivery.

## FP&A Recommendation

Management should not evaluate the growth strategy based on revenue alone.

The key question is whether incremental customers and orders generate sufficient contribution to support sustainable profitability.

Before increasing growth spending further, management should separate temporary strategic investment from structural cost deterioration and evaluate the unit economics of customer acquisition, fulfilment, and delivery.
